# Career Context

Following my first engagement at Uber, I began pursuing consulting roles that exposed me to new business domains and engineering challenges. Although my time at Restoration Hardware was brief, it provided an opportunity to apply distributed systems and backend engineering principles in a retail environment while quickly adapting to an unfamiliar codebase and business domain.

---

# Executive Summary

At Restoration Hardware, I worked on a proof-of-concept application for warehouse capacity planning. The project focused on helping distribution centers better understand and manage warehouse utilization by modeling capacity constraints and operational workflows.

As a short consulting engagement, my role was to quickly become productive, contribute to the proof of concept, and deliver value within a limited timeframe.

---

# Responsibilities

- Developed backend services supporting warehouse capacity planning
- Contributed to proof-of-concept implementation
- Collaborated with engineers and product stakeholders
- Worked within an existing distributed system architecture
- Delivered features within an aggressive project timeline

---

# Key Takeaways

Although the engagement lasted only a few months, it reinforced several important lessons:

- The ability to become productive quickly in unfamiliar systems is one of the most valuable consulting skills.
- Strong engineering fundamentals transfer effectively across industries and business domains.
- Understanding the underlying business problem is often more important than familiarity with a particular technology stack.
